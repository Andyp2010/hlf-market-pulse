Monthly & 10-year KiwiSaver significant financial hardship withdrawals

Ticker: kiwisaver-hardship-withdrawals • Latest Underlying Data: September 2026 • Last Checked: 10 November 2026 • Creator: Andy Palliser
Also known as: IRD KiwiSaver financial hardship withdrawal count, value & average
In September 2026, Inland Revenue recorded 5,440 KiwiSaver members approved for a significant financial hardship withdrawal totalling $47.9m—an HLF-derived mean of $8,805 per member (with annual hardship withdrawals rising over 10 years from 10,670 members / $59.4m in FY2016 to 52,330 members / $538.2m in FY2026).
Official dataInland Revenue KiwiSaver Withdrawals (September 2026): Financial hardship withdrawals = 5,440 members and $47.9m (FY2026: 52,330 / $538.2m vs FY2016: 10,670 / $59.4m).
HLF calculation$47.9m ÷ 5,440 members = $8,805/member (FY2026 annual avg: $10,285/member)
HLF interpretationHLF market context: Significant financial hardship withdrawals require supervisor approval when a member cannot meet minimum living expenses or mortgage arrears. Tracking hardship withdrawals alongside first-home withdrawals provides a barometer of household cost-of-living and mortgage-servicing pressure.

Data Quality & Limitations

  • Official published sources: Pulse uses official published data from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ), Stats NZ, Inland Revenue (IRD), the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), MBIE Tenancy Services, the Insurance Council of New Zealand (ICNZ), the Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake (NHC), and the Accident Compensation Corporation (ACC) wherever available.
  • Unaltered official figures: HLF does not alter or estimate official source figures.
  • Explicit derivation labels: Derived statistics (such as percentages, averages, rolling sums, multi-year compound growth rates, and cohort comparisons) are calculated directly from the underlying official series and identified as HLF-derived.
  • Price indices vs. individual quotes: Stats NZ CPI insurance sub-indices measure percentage changes in standardised insurance price indices over time—they do not represent the dollar premium quote for an individual property or policyholder.
  • Reporting lags & differing schedules: Reporting periods differ across agencies (for example, RBNZ lending series publish monthly with a ~4-week lag, MBIE bond data publishes on a ~2-month lag, Stats NZ CPI and IRD student loan balances publish quarterly, and FMA KiwiSaver reports, RBNZ Financial Stability Reports, ICNZ market data, ACC reports, and regional KiwiSaver breakdowns publish semi-annually or annually).
  • Commitments vs. settlements: RBNZ mortgage lending tables (C31, C32, C33, C40, C41) measure firm bank mortgage commitments/approvals rather than LINZ property title settlements.
  • Publisher revisions: Underlying historical figures may subsequently be revised by the original publisher in later releases.
  • Automatic staleness exclusion: HLF automatically excludes any series where the underlying source is unavailable, stale past its freshness window, or cannot be verified.
  • Market context vs. individual outcomes: Statistics describe aggregate historical market cohorts and do not predict an individual borrower’s, saver’s, or policyholder’s credit assessment, underwriting, or lending outcome.

About the HLF Pulse & Creator

HLF Pulse is a public reference dataset covering New Zealand mortgage, housing, KiwiSaver, tenancy, and protection statistics, published by HLF (Home Loan Factory) (HLF Financial Limited (FSP1008571)) and created by Andy Palliser (Andrew Jonathan Palliser, FSP1004725), Mortgage & KiwiSaver Adviser at Home Loan Factory.

Data is compiled from official releases from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, Stats NZ, Inland Revenue, the Financial Markets Authority, MBIE Tenancy Services, the Insurance Council of New Zealand, the Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake, and ACC. Where HLF calculates percentages, averages, or comparisons from the underlying official data, these are identified as “Derived by HLF”.

HLF does not alter or estimate official statistics. Where source data is unavailable, stale, or cannot be verified, the relevant series is excluded until it can be confirmed.
