For the 2026/27 tax year, the statutory ACC Earners' Levy is $1.75 per $100 (1.75% incl. GST) (up from $1.67 in 2025/26) up to a Maximum Liable Earnings cap of $156,641 (up from $152,790, for a maximum annual levy of $2,741.22)—setting ACC's maximum 80% weekly compensation ceiling at an HLF-derived $125,312.80/year ($2,409.86/week gross).
Official dataACC / IRD 2026/27 Schedule: Earners' levy = $1.75 per $100 incl. GST ($1.52 excl. GST); Maximum liable earnings = $156,641 (vs $152,790 in 2025/26).

HLF interpretationHLF market context: This affects borrowers in two ways: (1) In mortgage servicing calculators, the 1.75% ACC levy reduces net take-home pay on earnings up to $156,641; and (2) For an eligible accident, ACC caps income replacement at 80% of earnings up to $156,641 ($125,313/yr gross)—leaving higher-income earners with a larger percentage drop in income, and self-employed company owners splitting shareholder salaries potentially under-covered unless structured via ACC CoverPlus Extra or private cover.

acc-weekly-compensation-claims
ACC annual injury claims vs weekly income compensation claims (accident vs illness gap)
Also: ACC Annual Report total claims vs weekly compensation claims
Derived by HLF		ACC Annual Report
Data: 2025/26 ACC Financial Year
Checked: 10 November 2026
In the 2025/26 financial year, ACC accepted over 2,100,000 new injury claims across New Zealand (up 1.6% year-on-year), of which 105,000 claims—an HLF-derived 5.0% share (down 1.9% from 107,000 in 2024/25)—received weekly income compensation for time off work alongside $5.0B+ in treatment and rehabilitation support.
Official dataACC Annual Report (2025/26): Total accepted injury claims = 2,100,000+ (+1.6% YoY); New weekly compensation claims = 105,000 (-1.9% from 107,000 in 2024/25).
HLF calculation105,000 weekly compensation claims ÷ 2,100,000 total accepted injury claims = 5.0%
HLF interpretationHLF market context: Only 5.0% of accepted ACC claims involve weekly income compensation, and under the Accident Compensation Act 2001, ACC only covers physical injuries caused by an accident. ACC provides 0% cover if a homeowner cannot work due to non-injury illness (such as cancer, heart attack, stroke, mental health conditions, or degenerative illness)—which is the gap addressed by private Income Protection, Mortgage Repayment, and Trauma insurance.
Source: Accident Compensation Corporation (ACC) — Annual Report 2025/26. Weekly compensation share derived by HLF for general information only. • Open Data Dictionary page →

Data Quality & Limitations

  • Official published sources: Pulse uses official published data from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ), Stats NZ, Inland Revenue (IRD), the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), MBIE Tenancy Services, the Insurance Council of New Zealand (ICNZ), the Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake (NHC), and the Accident Compensation Corporation (ACC) wherever available.
  • Unaltered official figures: HLF does not alter or estimate official source figures.
  • Explicit derivation labels: Derived statistics (such as percentages, averages, rolling sums, multi-year compound growth rates, and cohort comparisons) are calculated directly from the underlying official series and identified as HLF-derived.
  • Price indices vs. individual quotes: Stats NZ CPI insurance sub-indices measure percentage changes in standardised insurance price indices over time—they do not represent the dollar premium quote for an individual property or policyholder.
  • Reporting lags & differing schedules: Reporting periods differ across agencies (for example, RBNZ lending series publish monthly with a ~4-week lag, MBIE bond data publishes on a ~2-month lag, Stats NZ CPI and IRD student loan balances publish quarterly, and FMA KiwiSaver reports, RBNZ Financial Stability Reports, ICNZ market data, ACC reports, and regional KiwiSaver breakdowns publish semi-annually or annually).
  • Commitments vs. settlements: RBNZ mortgage lending tables (C31, C32, C33, C40, C41) measure firm bank mortgage commitments/approvals rather than LINZ property title settlements.
  • Publisher revisions: Underlying historical figures may subsequently be revised by the original publisher in later releases.
  • Automatic staleness exclusion: HLF automatically excludes any series where the underlying source is unavailable, stale past its freshness window, or cannot be verified.
  • Market context vs. individual outcomes: Statistics describe aggregate historical market cohorts and do not predict an individual borrower’s, saver’s, or policyholder’s credit assessment, underwriting, or lending outcome.
